# Features

The feature specification of HCFCore: what each system does, the rules it follows, and why it is built that way. It is written for contributors and for server owners who want the reasoning behind a behaviour. For how to *use* the plugin, see the documentation in [`docs/`](docs/index.md), published at **https://lawkeys-dev.github.io/hcf-core/**.

Comments in the source refer to the numbered sections below (for example *"FEATURES.md section 3"*); the numbering is kept stable for that reason.

Every feature below is implemented. Where a value is a balance decision for the server — a points scale, a reward table, enchantment caps — the plugin ships the mechanism with an empty or neutral value, and nothing is decided in the operator's place.

---

## 1. Teams (factions)

- **Create, disband, rename.**
- **Roles**: leader, co-leader and member, with promotion and demotion. The minimum role for **every** action is configurable in `teams.yml` (`required-roles`), and so is the co-leader cap.
- **Invite, revoke, kick, leave.** Invitations expire after a configurable delay (`invite-expiry-seconds`, `0` = never).
- **Team chat** (`/team chat`): team and ally channels. Routing lives in `chat/listener/ChatListener`, **the single owner of `AsyncChatEvent` for the whole plugin**: two listeners on the same event (one cancelling, one formatting) would depend on their relative priorities. A player with no team talks in public rather than into the void.
- **Team info** (`/team info [team]`) and roster (members by role, how many online).
- **Alliances** (ally/unally), with a two-step handshake (a request, then an acceptance) and a configurable cap checked **on both sides**.
- **Focus** on an enemy player or team, shown on the scoreboard and, for Lunar Client players, as a waypoint and nametag colour.
- **`/team stuck`**, available **without a team** — a teamless player walled into someone else's claim is the one who needs it most. A ring search around the player (`StuckSearch`, pure and tested): the destination is always the nearest possible, not a random spot on the map. A player who is not stuck is told so instead of being moved.
- **Team ranking** (`/team list`, sorted by points, server teams excluded).
- **Team bank**: balance, deposit, withdraw. Moving money crosses two ledgers (the player's wallet and the team bank): if the second refuses, the first is rolled back through a restore path that obeys no setting, so a `/hcf reload` landing between the two halves cannot destroy money.
- **Leadership transfer** (the old leader drops to the role set in `role-after-leadership-transfer`).
- **Safe zones take no damage at all** (`pvp.yml`, `safe-zones.no-damage`, `keep-fed`, `heal`, all on as shipped, the owner's choice of 22/09/2026): on a safe server team's land nothing hurts a player - fall, fire, drowning, suffocation, mobs - and health and hunger are filled back up as they arrive.
- **Spawn is closed while you are in combat** (`safe-zones.block-combat-tagged`): a combat tag refuses the step into a safe zone, by walking, pearl or any other jump, until it runs out - a fight is not ended by reaching spawn. The border they may not cross is shown as a wall (`safe-zones.wall`: red glass, 15 blocks each way in front of them, to layer 128), drawn by the claim module through a seam (`SafeZoneWallPolicy`) - the same machinery as a locked claim's wall, sent to that player alone. Spawn is where players sort themselves out between fights, not somewhere to drown in the fountain.
- **Server teams (server land).** A server team is either a **safe zone** (spawn: no PvP) or a **combat zone** (warzone, roads, event grounds: PvP on); either way, no building and no player claims. `/team createsystem` hands over the claiming wand at once: server land is drawn like any claim, and an event's territory is a server team too (`/events claim`). Staff commands: `/team createsystem <name> <safe|combat>`, `/team setzone <team> <safe|combat>`, and `/team forceclaim|forceunclaim` to give it land (section 2). Excluded from rankings, protected from any change by a player, with no claim allowance or placement rules.
- **Staff overrides** (forcedisband, forcejoin, forcekick, forcepromote, forcedemote), through a `null` actor handed to the manager, which skips the rank checks — rather than duplicating each operation. Permission: `hcfcore.team.admin`.
- **Rally**: `/team rally` and `/team unrally`, with a configurable lifetime (`0` = until cleared), expiring lazily on read.
- **Team Points**: a score separate from the balance, with a configurable floor, a staff override (`/team setpoints`) and a relative adjustment (`/team addpoints`). **The scale is entirely configurable** in `teams.yml` — `per-kill` (to the killer's team, for a player of another team or of none), `per-death` (to the victim's team, any death), `per-raidable` (to a team its DTR just made raidable), `per-conquest-win`, `per-king-win`, and `koth.points-per-capture` (KOTH and Citadel). Killing a teammate or yourself earns nothing, and the floor holds. **Everything ships at `0`**: the scale is a balance decision for the server (the file warns about farming on teamless alts if `per-kill` is positive). Pure, tested rules (`TeamManager#recordDeath` and the others).
- **KOTH captures per team**: counted with a configurable cap (`TeamManager#recordKothCapture`), reset for a new map or period with `/team resetkoth`. Cap `0` (unlimited) by default, and no automatic reset.

## 2. Territory and claims

- **Claims drawn block by block with the claiming wand** - the traditional HCF claim, the project owner's choice of 22/09/2026 over the chunk claims of 0.7. `/team claim` hands over the wand (a golden hoe, `claims.yml` `wand`): left-click a block for the first corner, right-click one for the second, sneak + left-click to claim the rectangle between them - both corners included, full height - drop it to give up. The corners show as glass columns sent to the holder only (`sendBlockChange`, put back as they were); once both are picked, the chat gives the size, the price and, **before** confirming, any refusal (`ClaimManager#refusal`, the same checks the claim runs). The wand never leaves its holder: marked in its data (never recognised by name), kept out of containers, not dropped on death, and a wand with no selection behind it - after a restart - is simply taken back. `/team unclaim` gives up the whole claim one stands in; `/team unclaimall` everything.
- **Paid from the team bank** (`price.per-block`, `0.25` by default: a 20 x 20 claim is 100), and **money is the limit** - the owner's choice: no allowance by member count any more. A claim the bank cannot pay is refused and costs nothing. Unclaiming refunds `price.refund-percent` (75 %) of **what was paid** - stored with the claim, so a price raised later cannot be sold back dear. Staff claims and server land are free; a staff unclaim refunds nothing.
- **Sizes** (`sizes`): at least 5 x 5 (a one-block strip around an enemy base is not territory), at most 128 a side, and optional caps on the number of claims and on the total surface (`0` = none). Staff and server land follow none: a road is three blocks wide.
- **Placement rules**: territory must be **connected** - a new claim shares an **edge** with the team's land in that world, a corner is not enough (per world - a team may open a territory in a world where it holds nothing) - a **buffer** of `buffer-blocks` (8) between teams, measured as the land between two rectangles (server land is no neighbour), and optionally no splitting one's own territory in two.
- **Converted from 0.7 automatically**: on the first start, each team's chunk claims become rectangles covering exactly the same land (`LegacyChunkClaims`, greedy row-then-column merging, pure and tested), paid nothing, saved at once; the chunk rows are then emptied, so an older server reading the database finds no stale claims.
- **Owner lookups stay allocation-free on the hot path**: claims are indexed by the chunks they cover, even in part, so "who owns this block" tests the few rectangles of one chunk, never every claim on the server; the per-chunk lists are replaced whole, never changed, so a lookup never sees one half-written. No overlap is possible: the manager refuses a claim over any other before it is stored.
- **HQ** (`/team sethq`, `/team hq`) — the home must be inside the team's land (configurable) — and a **second base** (`/team setbase`, `/team base`).
- **Lockclaim.** The leader and co-leaders can close **access** to their land to everyone but members, **during SOTW only** — so nobody camps inside a claim until SOTW ends. `/team lockclaim` (alias `/team lock`, role `lock-claim` in `claims.yml`, co-leader by default) toggles the lock; **allies included**; walking in is refused at the claim's border, and so are ender pearls and chorus fruit; non-members already inside are moved to the nearest free land (the `/team stuck` search, shared). `claim/` does not know what SOTW is: it declares the `LockWindow` seam (closed by default), which `phase/` opens during SOTW. **When SOTW ends, every lock lapses**, and the next SOTW starts open. Memory only: a restart releases the locks (a SOTW lasts hours, not a map). The players it refuses see a **wall of red glass** around the claim while the lock holds - appearing within `show-within-blocks` (5) blocks of it, its border near them, sent to each of them alone, never placed (`lock.wall` in `claims.yml`: block, height, how much of the border, or off). Each claim's wall is read from the world once and kept, so a step only crops it to what is near and sends the difference: it is redrawn as the player walks, not on a timer.
- **Territory map** (`/team map`, the owner's choice of 22/09/2026): columns in the world - ground to layer `map.pillars.top-y` (128) - on the corners of every player team's claim within `map.pillars.radius-chunks` (4) chunks (`include-system-claims` adds server land), one ore or block of ore per team, drawn at random each time and named in the chat under the map - shown to that player alone, like the claiming wand's, and gone after `map.pillars.seconds` or at the next `/team map`, which is the switch. The chat lists each block and whose land it marks. `/team map chat` (or `map.style: chat`) draws the old text grid instead: a character per `map.chat.cell-blocks` (8) blocks, coloured by relation (own / ally / enemy / server / warzone / wilderness), readable on every client.
- **Warzone**, configured in `claims.yml` (`warzone`), **off by default**. A square around the centre of each listed world (radius in blocks, exact - block-precise like claims): PvP on, no player claims, and no building unless `allow-building` allows it — explosions included; interactions (doors, chests) stay free. **Claimed land always wins**: spawn at the centre stays safe, a claimed road keeps its rules, a team that owned land there before keeps it. A Mountain inside the warzone stays minable, its own rules applying inside its region. Announced when crossed, shown by `/team here` and `/team map`.
- **Staff claims**: `/team forceclaim <team>` hands staff the wand for any team (or, with `<x1> <z1> <x2> <z2> [world]`, claims that rectangle at once - from the console too, so server land can be scripted) - free, outside the size and placement rules, never over anybody's land - and `/team forceunclaim <team> [all]` (permission `hcfcore.claim.admin`); this is how server land gets its land. The same wand draws event zones (`/events setzone <id>`, section 5).
- **`/team here`** (alias `claiminfo`): the owner, the claim's size and corners, protection state and territory (claims and blocks).
- **Griefing protection outside raids**: block break, block place, buckets and interactions refused in protected territory, with an explicit message (a silently cancelled event looks like a server bug). Staff bypass: `hcfcore.claim.bypass`, together with `/staffbuild` (section 8).
- **Entering and leaving territory** is announced (configurable), evaluated only when a player moves to another block column - borders run between blocks.
- **Land released automatically** when a team is disbanded, through the public `TeamDisbandEvent`.

## 3. DTR (Deaths Till Raidable)

- **DTR per team. DTR is a function of time, not a value a task updates**: what is stored is the value at the last death plus the instant regeneration resumes, and the current figure is computed on read. Three consequences: nothing drifts when a tick is missed, regeneration keeps running while the server is down (the usual HCF behaviour), and everything is testable by moving a clock instead of waiting.
- **Maximum tied to team size** (`base` + `per-member`, hard cap), loss per death, configurable floor. Defaults on the classic HCF scale: 1.1 DTR per player capped at 6.6, a death costs 1.0. **An intended consequence of this asymmetry: a solo team survives its first death** (1.1 − 1.0 = 0.1), and the second one opens its base.
- **Configurable regeneration**: frozen after a death (45 minutes by default), then +0.1 every 3 minutes. **In steps, not continuously**, so what players see matches what the server announces.
- **Staff overrides**: `/team setregen <team> <seconds>` and `/team setdtr <team> <value>`. `setdtr` does not restart the freeze: staff raising DTR to close a raid need not restart a 45-minute counter.
- **Raidable state shown**: `/team dtr [team]` (value, maximum, freeze left, time until protection returns), `/team here`, the scoreboard.
- **Announcements**: the cost of a death to the team, and becoming raidable / protected again to the whole server. Public event `TeamRaidableEvent` (ARCHITECTURE.md section 7).
- **The reclaim system**:
  - While a team's **DTR is at 0 or below**, its land is **raidable**: building and breaking protection is lifted inside its territory, so attackers can build and break freely (pillage).
  - As soon as the **DTR regenerates above 0**, the team **gets its protection back automatically** (building and breaking refused again for non-members) **and keeps its claim** — land never changes owner during this cycle.
  - **No over-claiming, ever**: at no point, not even during the raidable window, can another team claim an existing team's land. A raid gives access to the contents, never to ownership of the land.
  - Technically, the claim system tells apart "owner of the land" (persistent data) and "protection active" (a live state derived from the current DTR) — the build check reads the protection state, not a notion of "free claim".
  - **As built**: `claim/` asks a `RaidabilityPolicy` interface on every check; `dtr/` provides the implementation at startup. The `ReclaimLoop` test group wires the two real managers together and checks the whole cycle — opening at the death, closing only at regeneration, ownership intact throughout.

## 4. PvP and combat

- **Deathban**: configurable length, with **tiers by permission**. **The shortest tier wins**, so granting a rank is always a reduction and never accidentally a punishment; only the nodes listed in the configuration are checked, so an unrelated wildcard cannot silently shorten a ban. Stored as an expiry instant, not a remaining duration — a deathban that paused over a restart would be trivially bypassed. The login check reads the in-memory cache, since a login cannot wait on a query. **Staff bypass** `hcfcore.deathban.bypass` is read **at the death**, where the player is still on the server: a staff member holding it is never banned, instead of being banned and then let back in. The login check needs no permission at all — the connection carries a profile, not a player, and Bukkit has no offline permission API. The price, accepted: a player granted the node *while* banned stays out, since the ban was written before they had it; `/pvp lift <player>` lifts it, from the console too.
- **Combat tag** (`/pvp`): configurable length, both sides tagged (configurable), refreshed on every hit without re-spamming the player. Projectiles trace back to the shooter, so an archer is tagged. **Not persisted** on purpose: a tag lasts a few dozen seconds, and making it survive a restart would punish players for the server being down.
- **Item cooldowns** (`pvp.yml`, `item-cooldowns`): a wait between two uses of any item listed - eaten, or a totem saving its holder; as shipped the Gapple (1 h), the Crapple (10 s), the chorus fruit (15 s) and the totem (2 min). A use meanwhile is refused - at `LOW`, before the classic combat eats a golden apple its own way, which then starts the cooldown itself (`PvpModule#itemUsed`); a totem on cooldown saves nobody. **Kept in the player's own data** (`PersistentDataContainer`), so an hour's Gapple survives logouts and restarts without a table of its own. Shown on the scoreboard (`%cooldown_<id>_line%`), greyed out on the item (reapplied at login, the game forgetting it) and on Lunar Client. Partner items are exempt, and their right-click and hit items carry a cooldown group of their own (`use_cooldown`), so a Crapple's greyed-out golden apples never lock a Golden Head.
- **`/cooldown reset <player> [what]`** (`hcfcore.cooldown.admin`, from the console too): ends a player's cooldowns whichever module keeps them - `all`, `abilities`, `global`, `pearl`, `items`, or one ability's or item's id.
- **Ender pearl cooldown** (`pvp.yml`, `ender-pearl-cooldown`): 15 seconds between two pearls as shipped; a pearl thrown meanwhile is refused and kept. Shown on the scoreboard (`%pearl_line%`), on the item (the game's own cooldown display, set after its one-second one and the classic combat's clearing of it) and on Lunar Client. A death ends it; a logout does not - leaving and coming back is no way around it. With `block-teleport`, it refuses plugin teleports as a combat tag does, through the same `TeleportGuard` the PvP module answers (`/spawn`, `/team hq`, `/team stuck`, `/top`, `/world`). Memory only, as the combat tag. A partner item's Fake Pearl is exempt, through a seam the ability module installs (`PvpModule#setPearlExempt`).
- **Combat logging**: a tagged player who disconnects dies. The death follows the normal path, so the deathban applies and the team loses DTR.
- **Hits between teammates and allies**: **teammates never hurt each other; allies only inside an event area** — the zone of a running KOTH, Citadel, Conquest, DTC, Last Break or Slide, or a hit on (or by) the King during Kill the King — which is where allied teams compete, captures and the King being strictly per team. A refused hit does nothing and tags nobody. Configurable (`pvp.yml`, `friendly-fire`: `teammates`, `allies: ALWAYS|EVENT_AREAS|NEVER`). Pure, tested rule (`FriendlyFire`); "where are the event areas" is a `pvp/` seam (`AllyCombatZone`) that `events/` fills. Mountains are not event areas: nobody captures a node.
- **Strength nerf**: the vanilla bonus is subtracted and replaced by the configured one. ⚠️ **The vanilla figure is a setting, not a constant**: Mojang has changed the formula before (a multiplier before 1.9, a flat bonus after), and the value for the targeted version could not be confirmed in the official documentation. **Check it in `pvp.yml` for your version** — no recompile needed. The effect itself is read through `PotionEffectType.STRENGTH`, verified in the 26.2 sources. Only a blow (`ENTITY_ATTACK` by the player): an arrow never carried Strength, and the nerf used to take it out of one.
- **Safe zones**: PvP is off on the land of server teams created as **safe zones**; those created as **combat zones** (warzone, roads, event grounds) are fought on like anywhere else. Both are defined like any territory, by claiming chunks for a server team. The warzone radius of `claims.yml` (section 2) is a combat zone.
- **Teleports blocked in combat**: `/team hq` refuses to teleport a tagged player, through a `TeleportGuard` seam that `pvp/` fills at startup — `claim/` gates on combat without knowing what combat is.
- **Teleport countdowns** for `/team hq`, `/team base` (10 s by default) and `/team stuck` (60 s), as for `/spawn` and `/logout`. Any damage or change of block cancels it; the combat guard is **asked again at the end** (hitting someone tags the attacker without hurting them, so no damage cancels their countdown). Countdowns live in a shared `warmup/` module — `general/` can be switched off as a whole, so `claim/` could not depend on it — with one countdown at a time per player. Teleports use `teleportAsync`, and the landing height of `/team stuck` is read after the chunk is loaded asynchronously.

## 5. Economy

- **Player balance and `/pay`**: `/balance [player]` (aliases `bal`, `money`) and `/pay <player> <amount>`. An account never touched reports the starting balance **without a row being written**: a player who never earns costs no storage. Transfers can be switched off independently of the rest of the economy, with a configurable minimum.
- **Staff economy** (`/eco give|take|set`), permission `hcfcore.economy.admin`. The target is told about the change: a balance that changes silently reads as a bug.
- **Money safety**: every change goes through `ConcurrentHashMap#compute`, which holds the bin lock during the read *and* the write: two simultaneous withdrawals cannot spend the same money twice. Covered by two load tests (16 and 32 threads) checking that exactly the expected number of withdrawals succeeds and that the total money supply is conserved.
- **Vault bridge (`Economy`)**, a soft dependency: without Vault, the internal economy works fully, it is simply not exposed to other plugins. Signatures **checked against the source** (`github.com/MilkBowl/VaultAPI`, tag `1.7`, the one pinned in `build.gradle.kts`). A pure adapter: no rule lives there, so a third-party shop gets exactly the same ceiling, refusals and concurrency guarantees as `/pay`.
  - **Vault banks are deliberately not team banks**: `hasBankSupport()` returns `false`. Vault's `bank*` calls carry no actor, so wiring them would let any installed plugin move a team's money **without any rank check**.
  - Balances are not split by world: Vault's `worldName` overloads ignore the argument rather than pretending to a per-world ledger the storage does not have.

## 6. Faction events

- **KOTH (King of the Hill)**: a timed zone capture. A team alone in the zone runs the countdown down; an enemy present freezes it. A cuboid zone set by two corners (in any order), capture time, contest policy, announcements at marks, daily schedule, rewards as console commands — all configuration.
  - **Configurable contest policy**: `RESET` (the classic, brutal one — losing the zone sends the countdown back to full) or `PAUSE` (it freezes and resumes for the next holder). It is the setting that changes most how a KOTH plays.
  - **Announcements only at transitions**, never every tick: a thirty-second brawl in the zone produces one message, not thirty.
- **Citadel** (its own section, `citadels:`): a KOTH with a much longer hold — `capture-seconds: 1800`, 30 minutes — fought inside a **Citadel**, a large area where the fight is decided by teams and classes and nobody gets away by throwing an item. **Two zones**: the **zone to hold**, a cuboid read exactly like a KOTH's and run by the same capture engine (the `CitadelIsJustConfiguration` test group checks that the long hold works on it without forcing it, ARCHITECTURE.md section 9); and **the Citadel itself**, server land — the claim of a server team, named by `claim`, created with `/team createsystem <name> combat` and `/team forceclaim` — so it is protected from building, shown on `/team map` and announced at its border like any server land. On that land, **at all times**, whether the event runs or not, `restrictions` refuse **ender pearls** thrown from inside, **partner items** (kit abilities), **chorus fruit** (refused before it is eaten, and any food that teleports), **elytra** (no take-off, and a player gliding in lands) and **Riptide tridents** — each with its own switch. **Class abilities keep working** (Bard, Archer, Rogue): they are not partner items. Items are refused, never the block clicked. Refusals are messages throttled by `RefusalThrottle`. The console warns when a Citadel starts without its claim, or with its zone to hold outside it — checked at the start, since teams and claims load after the settings. `events/` recognises a partner item through a seam the kit module fills (ARCHITECTURE.md section 14). Shipped staff-started until given a schedule.
- **Conquest**: the classic HCF Conquest (`events/conquest/`). Several zones captured **in parallel**, each like a KOTH (held alone by one team, frozen when contested, RESET/PAUSE when lost) but on a short timer; **a capture ends nothing** — it gives the team points and the zone's countdown restarts, the same team scoring as long as it holds. First team to `points-to-win` wins, and its rewards run. **A member's death, anywhere, costs the team `death-penalty` points** (never below zero). One Conquest at a time; ids shared with the captures and Kill the King (`/events start|stop`), a line in `/events`, `%conquest_line%` and `%conquest_zone_1..4%` on the scoreboard. Only useful transitions are announced (start, capture, points lost, end): four zones of "contested" would drown the chat. In memory, like a KOTH: a restart ends it. The shipped example (4 zones, 30 s, 1 point, 250 to win, −20 per death) has no schedule.
- **DTC (Destroy The Core) and Last Break** (`events/core/`, one engine, `dtc:`/`last-break:` in `events.yml`): a block **core** inside a zone, broken by teams standing in it — a `BlockBreakEvent` on the exact block, judged `HIGH` (refuse or let through, no drops or XP) then `MONITOR` (record, announce, put back one tick later). **Permanent**: it reappears after every break and stays after the run ends - as `core.idle-material` (bedrock), the way a Totem's column stands between runs, and as `core.material` (end stone) while the event is on - protected from explosions and pistons at all times. **Per-team cooldown** (`break-cooldown-seconds`, `util/Cooldowns` keyed by team id) — another team is never blocked by it. DTC's `counter` chooses `SHARED` (common `breaks` health; most breaks of its own wins a tie by whoever reached that count first) or `PER_TEAM` (first to its own `breaks` wins at once); Last Break has no `counter` and is always common health, won by whoever lands the emptying break, even with fewer breaks than another team. Creative, spectator and teamless players never break the core — hardcoded. **One DTC or Last Break at a time — they share a single run slot, whatever their own ids** (a KOTH, a Conquest, a Slide and a DTC/Last Break can all run together — the families are independent), ids shared with every other family, `%dtc_line%`/`%dtc_team_line%`/`%last_break_line%` on the scoreboard, a hologram over the core. Staff lay a zone and its core out in-game (`/events create|claim|setzone|setblock|delete`, `hcfcore.events.admin`) — the **only** commands that ever rewrite `events.yml`, and only the one section they touch; a `claim/BreakAllowance` seam lets the core be broken through territory protection while a run is under way, and the console warns when the core is not on server land (`/events info` shows it too).
- **Slide** (`events/slide/`, `slide:` in `events.yml`): a zone where every team member present scores `points-per-player` for their team every `interval-seconds`, cumulative — three members score three times as fast — reported and caught up across irregular ticks (`SlideRun#consumeIntervals`, leftover milliseconds carried to the next tick). **A member's death, anywhere, while the Slide runs, costs the team `death-penalty` points** (never below zero; `announce-deaths: false` mutes the broadcast, never the penalty). First team to `points-to-win` wins; several crossing it on the same tick hand it to whichever is highest, an exact tie changes nothing. Creative, spectator and teamless players never score — hardcoded. One Slide at a time, `%slide_line%` and the live top 3 (`%slide_top_1..3%`) on the scoreboard, a hologram over the zone.
- **Totem and Mini Totem** (`events/totem/`, `totem:` in `events.yml`, the owner's event of 22/09/2026 - the classic of faction and HCF servers): a column of `height` blocks (5 for the Totem, 3 for the Mini Totem, one engine) standing in the event zone, **bedrock** between runs and **quartz** during one (`idle-material`, `material`). A team breaks the blocks **with a sword** (`tools`, every sword by default; each block mined the whole way as shipped; `instant-break: true` makes a hit break it at once through `BlockDamageEvent#setInstaBreak`), each broken block turning to bedrock (`broken-material`) and staying so; **the first team to break the whole column wins**. **A block broken by any other team starts the totem over** - every block quartz again - and that break counts for nobody (`rival-break: RESET`) or as the other team's first (`RESET_AND_START`). Creative, spectator and teamless players never break it; the column is protected from explosions and pistons, and outside a run only staff in creative can take it apart. The column is drawn from the run after every break and at start and end, so a reset, a win or a stop always come back to the same picture. Set up with `/events create totem|minitotem <id>` and `/events setblock <id>` (looking at the block the column stands on, which builds it of bedrock). `%totem_line%` on the scoreboard, a hologram above the column, a Lunar waypoint, `points.per-totem-win`, reward commands, schedule and `max-duration-seconds` as for every event. `TotemManager` pure and tested.
- **Setting up any event in game** (`events/setup/`, the owner's choice of 22/09/2026 - one way of doing things, so staff are not lost): the same verbs for every kind. `/events create <type> <id>` copies the shipped example of that kind to where the staff member stands - its block (core, column base) or the middle of its zones' floor on their position, no schedule - makes its server team (a combat zone named after it, in the `claim` key) and claims its zones plus `setup.claim-margin` blocks (`auto-claim`). `/events info <id>` is the checklist: territory (claims, area), each zone and whether it lies inside the territory, the core or the column (and whether it is built), the schedule, whether it runs - each line with its command. `/events claim|unclaim` draw and release the territory with the claiming wand; `/events setzone <id> [zone]` draws a zone (a Conquest's named one; a new name adds it), `delzone` removes one of a Conquest's; `/events setblock` places a core or a column; `/events delete` removes the event, a Totem's column, and disbands its server team unless another event names it. A running event is never edited. Kill the King has nothing to set up but its world: its ground is the warzone. What stays hard-coded: the templates are the jar's `events.yml`, never the server's.
- **The weekly schedule** (`events/planning/`, the owner's request of 22/09/2026): `weekly-schedule.days` in `events.yml` lists `"HH:mm <event id>"` starts per day of the week, alongside each event's daily `schedule`. `PlanningController`, ticked with the events, starts each through `EventLauncher` - the same code as `/events start`, whatever the engine - and announces every start, weekly or daily, `announce-before-minutes` ahead; a refused start is logged and skipped, and nothing missed while the server was down is caught up. `/schedule` lists the next seven days for everybody (`WeekAgenda`, pure, merging both sources); `/schedule add|remove` (`hcfcore.events.admin`) write the days into `events.yml`. Day names and the date format are in the language file. `/schedule` opens as a **window** - one item per day of the week ahead, that day's events hour by hour (`weekly-schedule.menu`); `/schedule chat` prints the list instead, and the console always gets it.
- **No abilities on an event's land** (the owner's request of 22/09/2026): `abilities.yml`'s `disabled-in.event-territory` refuses partner items on an event's territory - the land of the server team its `claim` names, or of one named after it - `during-event` (as shipped), `always` or `never` (`TerritoryAbilityMode`; the zone alone is `disabled-in.events`); an event's own `disable-abilities` overrides it for its land, and on land two events share a refusal by either wins (`events/setup/EventTerritories`, pure).
- **SOTW / EOTW (Start / End Of The World)**, module `phase/`, configured in `phases.yml`:
  - **SOTW**: **no PvP anywhere**, **no deathban**, **no DTR lost**. A player can **`/sotw enable`**: for the rest of that SOTW they hit and can be hit — only by those who did the same. **There is no going back**: fighting and then returning to SOTW's shelter would be an exploit. The no-PvP rule holds even with `pvp.yml` off: it is the map's rule.
  - **EOTW**: **every team is raidable** whatever its DTR, **no new player claims** (staff still claim server land), and **a death bans until the end of the map** — staff lift those bans after the reset. Spawn stays a safe zone.
  - **Starting**: staff commands (`/sotw start [duration]`, `/sotw stop`, `/eotw start|stop`, permission `hcfcore.phase.admin`) **and** optional dates in `phases.yml`. A scheduled SOTW is a window, from its date to its date plus its duration: a server that was down at the start gets what remains, and a date left over from an earlier map does nothing. The same guard applies to EOTW, through a configurable start window. **The state survives a restart.**
  - SOTW and EOTW never run together: one refuses to start during the other, and a scheduled EOTW that falls during a SOTW waits for its end, inside its window.
- **Kill the King** (`events/king/`, `kill-the-king:` in `events.yml`), started with `/events start <id>` or at fixed times like a KOTH:
  - **The event's arena is the warzone** of the chosen world (`claims.yml`), not a zone of its own: the two cannot diverge. The King is drawn at random among **online players** (survival or adventure, without `hcfcore.events.king.exempt`, a configurable minimum) and **sent to a random spot in the warzone**, on the surface, off claimed land. **Their position is shown to everybody**: on every scoreboard (`%king_location_line%`, redrawn with the board, every 20 ticks by default), as a waypoint for Lunar Client players, and in chat every `announce-interval-seconds` (60 by default) with the King's health as a percentage of their maximum (`KingHealth`, pure and tested). A chat line every second, as it first was, flooded the chat for the whole reign; an old `coordinates-interval-ticks` in a file is reported as no longer used.
  - **Their inventory is put aside in the database** (table `hcf_king_stashes`, written at the crowning) and **given back at the end** — at once if they survive or the event is stopped, at respawn if they die, at their next login if they leave or the server goes down. What they pick up during their reign is theirs to keep; only the kit is taken back. What drops at their death is the kit, and it is ordinary loot.
  - **The kit stays on the King**: during the reign they cannot drop anything, open a container, or use an entity (item frames, armour stands, allays). Otherwise, giving back their inventory would let them hand the kit to their team and keep their own items.
  - **The King cannot enter spawn by any means**: walking, pearls, chorus fruit, commands, portals — and cannot mount anything. A safe zone also counts as "outside the warzone": hiding there withers them.
  - **Who wins**: the King if alive at the end; otherwise the player credited with the kill, **provided they are not on the King's team** — neither at the crowning nor at the death (a King cannot leave their team to hand it the prize). Allies of the King's team are not excluded: the rule is strictly per team. Any other death (Wither, fall, lava, suicide) or a logout: **no winner**.
  - **Outside penalty**: a grace delay, damage per second, and a Wither whose level rises in steps up to a cap — the count starts over each time the King returns to the zone.
  - One Kill the King at a time.
- **Mountains (Glowstone Mountain, Ore Mountain)**, module `resourcenode/`. **Not a capture event** but a **resource refill**, inheriting nothing from `events/` — there is nothing to hold, nobody to declare the winner and nothing to reward.
  - A predefined region (often in the warzone or the Nether) **refills with specific blocks** at regular times.
  - **Refill times are absolute, not "last refill + interval"**: an interval is anchored to local midnight, so `interval-seconds: 14400` means 00:00, 04:00, 08:00… every day, whatever happened to the server in between. A restart cannot shift a mountain, players can learn the hours, and the next refill is shown without storing any state. Explicit times (`times`) can be added to the interval; both sources merge.
  - **Weighted palette**: `GLOWSTONE: 1` beside `NETHERRACK: 4` means one block in five. Weights are relative, nothing has to add up to 100. A plain list (equal weights) is accepted too. A block name the server does not know is **reported at startup and skipped**, never guessed.
  - **A refill repairs, it does not rebuild**: by default it only replaces air — what players mined. Extra materials can be declared replaceable.
  - **No building and no claiming in the region** — including by what nobody places by hand: a piston can neither push blocks in nor pull the structure out, and liquid poured outside does not flow in. No claiming goes through a `ReservedRegionPolicy` seam declared in `claim/`: the territory module never had to learn what a mountain is (ARCHITECTURE.md section 14).
  - **What can be mined is configurable**: `PALETTE_ONLY` (default — only the blocks the node places, so the structure survives and there is still a mountain to fill next time) or `ANY` (the region is then only non-buildable, and players will eventually flatten it).
  - **Explosions** cannot break the region (`prevent-explosions`), otherwise the mining rule would be decorative.
  - **Announced when the refill is done**, not when it starts (an announcement at the start sent teams to a half-empty mountain) — **and warnings ahead** (`announce-before-seconds`): the warning is what turns a refill into a rendez-vous rather than a surprise, since teams need time to cross the map. The warning is derived from the same calendar as the refill, so the two cannot diverge.
  - **The refill is spread over several ticks** (`blocks-per-tick`). A mountain is tens of thousands of blocks; placing them in one tick would freeze the server.
  - **No blocking chunk load**: a refill waits for its region's chunks to load asynchronously, then holds them with a ticket until it finishes, so none unloads — and must be reloaded on the main thread — along the way.
  - **A player standing in the region is not walled in** (`skip-occupied-blocks`, on by default): the blocks their body occupies stay empty until the next refill, instead of the resource closing over them.
  - **Refills appear in `/events`** next to the KOTHs (the single agenda of section 10), through an `AgendaContributor` seam. Staff verbs stay separate: `/resourcenode refill <id>`.
  - **No schematic support**: the region is a box. A free-form mountain would need a dependency (WorldEdit) or a file format of its own.
- **The Purge**: **a window of time during which every team is raidable**, whatever its DTR — a temporary EOTW, without its closed claims or its bans until the end of the map (deathbans and DTR as usual, spawn still safe). In `phase/`: the `RaidabilityPolicy` that EOTW already wrapped becomes "EOTW **or the Purge** or the DTR". `/purge start [duration]`, `/purge stop`, `/purge` for the status; optional daily times (`phases.yml`, empty by default) read by `DailySchedule` — a time passed while the server was off is not replayed. Like a SOTW, it ends at a stored instant: a restart does not cut it short. **Never during SOTW** (a SOTW that starts ends a running Purge: raidable claims on a map where nobody can fight make no sense), refused during EOTW (everything is raidable already). `%phase_line%` on the scoreboard and an entry in `/events`.
- **Allies and captures**: capture is **strictly per team**, for KOTH, Citadel, Conquest, DTC and Last Break alike (and scoring in Slide). Two allied teams contest each other; the engine does not consult alliances. Pinned by the test `twoAlliedTeamsStillContestEachOther`.

## 7. Kits, killstreaks, custom enchants

- **Kits** (module `kit/`). **A kit is created by saving an inventory, not by writing YAML**: `/kit create <id> [cooldown] [permission]` stores the staff member's inventory as it is (enchantments, potions, names), so `kits.yml` has no kit list to get wrong. The cooldown is stored as an **end instant** (it runs while the server is off, like a deathban: a daily kit would be worthless on a server that restarts every night); deleting a kit clears its cooldowns; `/kit give` starts none; overflow drops at the player's feet rather than being eaten when the kit does not clear the inventory first.
- **Killstreaks** (module `killstreak/`). The streak is counted and **persisted** by `stats/` (a streak reset by every restart could be farmed by waiting for one); `killstreak/` plugs into it through the `KillstreakObserver` seam. **One reward per kill, at the exact streak** — lower rungs were given on the way up. Rewards are console commands plus an optional announcement; **the table ships empty** (`killstreaks.yml`), for the same reason as the Team Points scale.
- **Custom enchants** (module `enchant/`): enchantments that do not exist in vanilla — **the well-known HCF and kitmap enchants**. **Five behaviours**, each a `type` in `enchants.yml`: `effect` (a potion effect while the piece is worn — never over a stronger effect from elsewhere, and removed only if it is still its own), `hellforged` (durability restored on each hit taken), `implanted` (food restored at an interval), `recover` (Regeneration below a health threshold, then a cooldown), `autosmelt` (a tool that smelts what it mines, **following the server's furnace recipes**, datapacks included). **Shipped configured**: Fire Resistance, Speed II, Jump Boost II, Night Vision, Invisibility, Hellforged IV, Implanted III, Recover II, Autosmelt — names, levels, pieces and numbers all editable; one more effect enchant is one more block of YAML. **The enchant lives on the item** (its level in the item's persistent data, a lore line, the enchantment glint): kits, chests, deaths and trades carry it like the rest of the item. It gets there through `/cenchant apply` (staff) or a **book dragged onto the item** in the inventory, with the anvil's rule (a higher book replaces, an equal one adds a level, up to the maximum); `/cenchant give <player> <enchant> [level] [n]` hands out books and works from the console — that is how a shop, a crate or a `/redeem` code gives them. Lowering a `max-level` lowers items already made.
- **Repair** (`general/`): `/repair` (alias `/fix`) repairs the held item, `/repair all` everything worn and carried, with **two separate permissions** (`hcfcore.general.repair`, `hcfcore.general.repair.all`) because they are two different perks. No cost and no cooldown: a rank cooldown that did not survive a restart would be worthless, and storing one is a gameplay decision to make first.
- **Abilities / partner items** (module `ability/`, `abilities.yml`): items that do something when used - right-clicked, thrown, hit with, shot from, reeled in. **38 types are built in**, and 42 abilities shipped with the values the owner chose: Switcher, Thunderbolt, Combo, Lucky Mode, Rage Ball, Crafting Chaos, Focus Mode, Ninja Track, Portable Archer, Invisibility, Time Warp, Pocket Bard, Berserk, Close Call, Switch Stick, Belch Bomb, Reverse Ninja Track, Rose Thorn, Pumpkin Reaper, Hulk Smash, Sticky Web, Med Kit, Grappling Hook, Nausea Axe, Bunny Hop, Ice Berg, Antidote, Golden Head, Grabber, Poisonous Potato, Fake Pearl, Rocket, Combo Fish, Anti-Build Bone, Rotten Egg, Rage Strength, Olympia, Baguette, Sun, Scrambler, Lucky Bard, Disarmer Wand. Generic types carry several of them - `effects` (Med Kit, Bunny Hop, Golden Head, Rage Strength), `hit-effects` (Nausea Axe, Ice Berg, Poisonous Potato), `thrown-effects` - and a `commands` type runs console commands for anything else. The behaviours are written from scratch - a commercial core's configuration was the reference for the list and the values, never code. **One gate for every use** (`AbilityModule#mayUse`): the module on, not where abilities are refused (`disabled-in`: a Citadel's claim, a running event's zone, the Nether, the End, optionally the warzone), no shared cooldown (10 s after any ability), not its own; nothing is spent on a refusal, while a chance that misses starts the cooldown but keeps the item and its uses. An enemy is reached only if `PvpModule#judgeHarm` lets a blow through (safe zones, SOTW, teammates, allies); a delayed teleport is cancelled if its target has since become unreachable. Items keep the key `hcfcore:ability` the kit module used, so partner items handed out before still work; `/ability give` replaces `/kit ability`. Choices made with the owner: Thunderbolt, the Sun and the Olympia's eggs take their damage through armour; the Sun catches and counts only the enemies in range, never the user nor a teammate; no ability works in a safe zone (`disabled-in.safezone`); the Pocket Bard's items reach the teammates within 20 blocks and the user; Invisibility hides the armour too, through `Player#sendEquipmentChange`, renewed each second for players who come near; the Pumpkin Reaper works on a Diamond only, and its pumpkin (Curse of Binding) is always traded back for the helmet - on its timer, a logout, a death's drops, a shutdown; the Disarmer Wand never drops the weapon; an item with `uses` (Olympia 30, Pumpkin Reaper and Nausea Axe 10, Portable Archer 5) stays and wears by one per use that works only - `PlayerItemDamageEvent` is cancelled for it, its durability bar being the count; the Baguette drains food through the game's Hunger (`AbilityRules#hungerAmplifier`), so the player can eat; the Grappling Hook pulls a tick after the reel, from the ground or mid-air, the hook held by a block it is stuck in, lies on or touches; the Fake Pearl has its own cooldown group (`use_cooldown`), so real pearls stay ready. The configuration is read by `AbilityConfig`, pure and tested - the shipped file must load all 42 without a warning - as are the rules (`AbilityRules`: the Sun's damage, the Shotgun's pellet cone, launch and pull velocities; `HitCounter`).
- **Refill signs (kitmap)**: a sign whose first line is `[Kit]` and second line the kit id (creating one needs `hcfcore.kit.sign`). An anti-spam wait of its own, **separate from the kit's cooldown**: a kitmap kit usually has no cooldown, and the sign still must not be clickable sixty times a second.

## 8. Staff tools and moderation

- **Mining alerts ("Found Diamonds")** (`staff/mining/`, `staff.yml` `mining-alerts`, the owner's request of 23/09/2026): a line to staff when a player breaks into a vein of a listed ore - diamond, ancient debris, emerald as shipped - once per vein, with its size (a flood fill over touching blocks, face or edge, capped at `max-vein`; `VeinCounter`, pure and tested) and position. Ores a player placed are never reported, and the rest of a reported vein stays quiet; both memories are bounded and in memory only. `audience: everyone` gives everybody the public "[FD]" line, never with the position.
- **Staff mode (`/staff`)**: staff get a **toolbar of items bound to commands**. Each slot of `staff.yml` has a material, a name, a lore, a command (without the `/`) and a `needs-target` - and optionally a `vanished-material`, shown while its holder is vanished, so the shipped Vanish switch is a grey dye that turns green (redrawn wherever it is, the moment vanish changes); the Random Teleport is an eye of ender; `%player%` becomes the player clicked, `%staff%` the staff member. The command **runs as the staff member**, so their own permissions apply. The mode brings **vanish + flight + invulnerability** (no damage taken or dealt, no mob aggro, no item pickup) — *not* building, which stays `/staffbuild`.
- **The survival inventory is persisted**, not kept in memory. Table `hcf_staff_stashes` in `MEDIUMBLOB`. Written **before** the inventory is emptied, given back **before** the mode flag drops, handed back at the next login if a crash came in between, and never overwritten by a second stash. It is the only state of the module that survives a restart; the rest (who is in the mode, who is hidden, who has the channel) is ephemeral on purpose, like a combat tag.
- **Vanish** (`/vanish`, alias `/v`, and automatically in staff mode). `hcfcore.staff.vanish.see` lets a player see vanished staff; **nobody holds it by default, operators included** — it is a decision about who is invisible to whom, and granting it to everyone would make vanish useless among staff. A vanish turned on by hand **survives leaving staff mode**; one set by staff mode leaves with it.
- **Freeze**, as a **dedicated command** (`/freeze`, alias `/ss`) and **not** through team `focus`: focus is a gameplay marker a team puts on an enemy, a moderation hold is not the same act. A frozen player can neither move, build, break, interact, drop, hit, be hit, nor run a command outside an allow-list — but **can look around and talk**, because a check is a conversation. **Disconnecting while frozen is a ban with no expiry**, lifted only by staff (`/freeze unban`). A configurable periodic reminder, so a player who cannot move does not think their game froze and restart their client — precisely the disconnect the hold tries to avoid.
- **Invsee**: **read-only by default, changes behind `hcfcore.staff.invsee.edit`**. On a server where items *are* the economy, an accidental drag in somebody's inventory is irreversible and looks exactly like staff theft. **The real inventory is opened, not a copy** — a copy would need a write-back when closed, which is exactly the duplication vector to avoid (two staff looking, or the player moving items meanwhile). The price: the window only shows the 36 storage slots, so armour and off-hand are shown as text.
- **Reports and requests, in one queue**: it is the same act with a different word on the front (someone wants staff attention, and here is why), and two systems would double commands, storage and review for nothing. `/report <player> <reason>` (online players only), `/request <message>`, a configurable per-player cooldown. **Management menu**: `/tickets` opens a chest listing the queue, oldest first; left-click takes the ticket and teleports to it (to the reported player, otherwise to whoever asked), right-click closes it. `/tickets list|view|claim|close <id>` do the same by number, from the console too. A ticket can be taken by one staff member at a time. Whoever raised it is told when it is taken and closed — **without the staff member's name**, who may be vanished while watching the reported player. Closed tickets stay in the database (`hcf_staff_tickets`) but leave memory. **A player cannot report someone they cannot see**: the answer is the same as for an offline player, otherwise `/report` would detect vanished staff (completion included).
- **Staff chat** (`/staffchat`, alias `/sc`) toggles the channel, or sends a single line when given a message — usable from the console. Configurable format.
- **Staff teleports**, as **subcommands of `/staff`** rather than `/tp`, `/near` and so on: those names are taken (`/tp` is vanilla, `/near` is one of the most claimed names on a Bukkit server), and claiming them would either lose to whichever plugin registered first or silently steal it from a plugin the operator wanted to keep. `/staff tp|tphere|tpall|tploc|tpnearest|randomtp|near|telllocation`.
- **Staffbuild** (`/staffbuild`, alias `/sb`) goes through territory protection via the `claim/BuildOverride` seam, **only with `hcfcore.claim.bypass` as well**. **Deliberately outside the staff mode bundle**: watching a raid and changing the world are two different intentions, and tying them would put every invisible staff member one click away from breaking a wall.
- **Lastinv**: the **last N deaths** per player (`keep`, 3 by default), always read-only: a snapshot records a past fact, and changing it would mean nothing. **The only storage in the project that is not cached**, on purpose: it is an archive read on demand, not live state; cached, it would be three full inventories for every player who ever played. Queried off the main thread, window opened on return.
- **Broadcast and clearchat**: `/broadcast` (`/bc`) and `/clearchat` (`/cc`), each with its own permission. Clearchat pushes blank lines (there is no API to clear a client's chat, the history lives on the client) and **skips staff**, who need to read what caused the clear; the number of lines is configurable.
- **End / Nether players**: `/staff end` and `/staff nether`, listed by `World.Environment` rather than by world name, so a server with several Nethers, or whose worlds do not carry the usual names, gets the right answer instead of a guess at "world_nether".
- **Strikes — sanctions against a TEAM, by offence** (the owner's rules of 19/09/2026): a strike is given for an **offence** - cheating (50% of the team's points), bug abuse, kill boosting (40%), teaming (35%), other (25%) as shipped - and **whatever the offences, the team is disbanded at its 3rd active strike** (`disband-at`, through the same path as `/team forcedisband`). `/strike add <team|player> <offence> [details]` (a player's name strikes their team and records the member), `/strike pardon <id>`, `/strike offences` (permission `hcfcore.staff.strike`; points taken and a disband are not given back), `/strike list [team|player]` open to everyone (their own team by default) with each strike's offence - its details for staff only. **Nothing is said in the general chat**: staff (and the console) are told of the strike, its offence and details, the points taken and a disband; the team's members are told when it disbands them; `/team show` writes `Strikes: N` from the first one, through a seam the staff module fills (`TeamModule#setStrikeCounter`). `staff.yml` holds the offences (`name`, `points-loss-percent`, `commands` with `%team%`/`%strikes%`/`%offence%`) and `disband-at`; `valid-seconds: 0` = the whole map; a strike keeps its offence's id (staff schema v7). A strike outlives its team: a disbanded team is found by the name it had. Pure, tested rules (`StrikeOffences`, `StrikeManager`). *Not covered*: a team on the edge of disbanding can disband and re-create itself - strikes follow the team, not its members; and no strike is added automatically (the plugin cannot know that a ban made by another plugin was for cheating).

## 9. UI and player feedback

- **Theme** (module `theme/`, `theme.yml`): one look for the whole plugin, chosen with the owner on 19/09/2026 ("Or royal"). **Eight colours by role** (primary, secondary, text, muted, dark, success, error, warning), hex, used through tokens - `{primary}`, `{muted}`... - in every shipped text: `lang/en.yml` (every message converted), the scoreboard, menus, holograms, chat format, staff toolbar. `{prefix}` (a bold `HCF »`) starts the announcements - events, phases, a team's news, strikes; `{bullet}` (`➥`) the scoreboard's `Label ➥ value` rows; `{action}` a menu item's "click to" line. Titles in small capitals (`small-caps-titles`). **Hex colours** everywhere: `&#rrggbb` in any text, read by `util/LegacyText` for components. **Gradients**: a role may be `#rrggbb>#rrggbb`, two stops or more; `Theme#apply` lays the text it colours out letter by letter, up to the next colour change, keeping formats on each letter (tested). **A whole look in one file**: `theme.yml` may carry `messages` (any `lang/en.yml` key, looked up before the language file), `chat` (`format`, `kills-format`, over `chat.yml`) and `nametags` (over `apollo.yml`), so a theme imports as one file. **Menus framed** (`frame`: full, bars, none; black panes, gold corners, no tooltip) with their items centred and, beyond one chest, **pages** with arrows (`theme/MenuLayout`, pure and tested): `/settings`, `/ability` (42 items: two pages), the Pocket Bard, `/dyes`, `/tickets` (paged instead of capped at 54), the kit layout editor. Relation colours (a nametag's ally or enemy, the claim map) stay semantic, and each ability keeps its own colour. A player's `{...}` in chat is escaped with the `&` codes. **The Theme Builder** (`docs/tools/theme-builder.html`, on the documentation site, presented by its own page and linked from the README and the home page) designs a theme with live previews in the game's typeface (Minecraft-Font, OFL, shipped with its licence), with the game's item textures and players' skins loaded from public services - never copied into the repository - chat, menus laid out by the same rules as `MenuLayout`, scoreboard, tab, Lunar nametags, a zone hologram - from 14 palettes, 9 of them gradients (the same gradient rules as `Theme`), and writes a complete `theme.yml` and `ui.yml`, one by one or zipped; it reads an existing `theme.yml` back, and `?palette=<id>` opens it on a palette. The documentation lists every interface setting, its file, and whether the builder covers it.
- **Scoreboard** (module `ui/`), on Paper's official scoreboard API: every line owns a fixed invisible entry whose team prefix alone is rewritten (no flicker), and `Objective#numberFormat` hides the red numbers. **The board is a list of template lines in `ui.yml`**, not a coded layout: a line whose placeholders all come out empty **disappears** (that is how the combat timer only shows in combat, and why a server without a module has nothing to edit), two identical lines stay distinct, 15 visible lines at most with a console warning beyond.
- **Item text is upright**: every name and lore line the plugin writes - menus (`/dyes`, `/settings`, tickets), the staff toolbar, partner items, the King's kit, the crowbar, enchant books, `/rename` - goes through `util/ItemText`, without the italics the game gives a custom name.
- **Tab list** (the project owner's request, 19/09/2026): two styles, chosen by `ui.yml`'s `style` - `auto` as shipped, the grid on an HCF server and the list on a kitmap.
  - **`hcf`, the HCF grid**: four columns of twenty cells in place of the player list - player info and location, team and members (online first, rank markers), server and events, top teams by points - every cell a template line. A cell whose placeholders are all empty is blank rather than dropped: the grid never shifts. **Heads**, in the style of the paid HCF cores (the owner cited Azurite): a cell may start with `[head:self]`, `[head:member:N]`, `[head:top:N]` (that team's leader) or `[head:<account>]` - any account's signed skin, the `MHF_` icons as shipped or `[head:mineskin:<id>]` - a mineskin.org skin by id or page link, fetched from its API (`HeadTag`, pure and tested). The Theme Builder lists the grid's heads, members' aside, in a section of their own, to set by name or mineskin.org link - and takes the shipped grid back for a grid saved in the browser before heads existed; its previews draw skins themselves, laid out as the game lays them out - a legacy 64x32 skin widened and its opaque hat dropped, the "Notch transparency hack" (Notch's own skin had a black helmet) - from skins kept in the page (the sample players, generated on mineskin.org, and the MHF icons), so the shipped look shows with nothing fetched; a head typed in is looked up through mineskin.org's API for a link or id, and Ashcon or PlayerDB for a name. A title's head is changed by clicking it in the preview. Grids saved in the browser by an earlier version of the page are set aside once for the shipped one. Online players' textures are read from their profile; others are asked of Mojang once, asynchronously, and cached. The game reads a head only when an entry is added, so a cell whose head changes is removed and added again. As shipped, heads mark only the category titles (MHF icons, the viewer's own beside Player Info) and the team's members (the owner's choice, 19/09/2026); every other cell shows a plain dark grey square - a Mojang-signed texture from mineskin.org's public gallery ("soju-tab-gray8"), since the client shows no unsigned skin in the list. With `skin` emptied, cells without a head all show the same default skin: their identities are chosen so that `floorMod(uuid.hashCode(), 18)` - how the game picks a default skin - falls on one, rather than a patchwork of nine faces. The game lays out 80 entries as 4 columns of 20, column by column, so the grid is 80 made-up entries with fixed identities ordered by list order (`ui/tab/TabGrid`, pure and tested), and the real players are taken off the viewer's list. Only changed cells are sent again. Counts and presence are the viewer's: `%online%` counts the players they can see, and a vanished teammate is offline to whoever cannot see them (found in game, 20/09/2026: `%online%` gave vanished staff away). **Needs nothing installed** (the owner's choice, 19/09/2026, over PacketEvents - GPL-3.0, which would have made a shaded jar GPL): Paper has no API for a list entry that is not a player, so `ServerGridTab` builds the server's two list packets by reflection, checked at start-up; on a version that reshaped them, the classic list, said once in the console. Real players come off the list through Paper's `unlistPlayer`, which the server honours in every later list packet; each redraw checks again, for a vanished player who could not be unlisted until seen.
  - **`classic`, the player list**: each real player written from `name` - their **LuckPerms prefix and suffix**, team, kills, ping - and ordered by `sort`: rank (the weight of their LuckPerms primary group), kills or name, through Paper's list order.
  - Each style has its own header and footer; the tab list's own placeholders (`%member_N%`, `%top_team_N%`, `%location%`, `%direction%`...) and its words (`lang/en.yml`, `ui.tab`). The header and footer used to be sent once, on joining, and never redrawn.
- **Leaderboards**: `/leaderboard` (alias `/lb`): kills, deaths, K/D, killstreak, playtime. Sorted from the cache on demand (a leaderboard is read far less often than a kill is recorded), ties broken by name. Shown in chat; a hologram leaderboard belongs to section 14.
- **Player stats (`/stats`)** (module `stats/`): kills, deaths, K/D (a player with no death is worth their number of kills, not infinity), current and best killstreak. The name is stored next to the UUID, so a leaderboard can show offline players without asking the server about them.
- **Playtime**: **accumulated, not derived** — a session's length is added at its end (deriving it from a first-join date would count years of absence; writing it during the session would count it twice — an SQLite test pins it), and shutting down closes every session before the final flush.
- **Player settings** (module `settings/`): `/settings` opens a menu (green = on, grey = off, a click toggles), `/settings <setting> [on|off]` and `/settings list` for those who prefer typing, and `/cobble`, the classic HCF shortcut. Four settings: **scoreboard**, **private messages** (the same switch as `/togglepm`, now kept), **tips**, **cobblestone pickup** (and cobbled deepslate, configurable) - and **the scoreboard section by section**: team, statistics, balance, combat tag, cooldowns, class, events, timers. A row of `ui.yml` belongs to a section by a tag at its start (`[team]`...), placed by the operator (`ui/ScoreboardRow`, tested with the shipped board); the settings module answers the scoreboard's row filter, a seam like its board filter. **Stored**: a player who turned the scoreboard off does not do it again after every restart; only settings switched *off* are written, so a default player costs no row. **No "sounds" setting**: the plugin plays no sounds, so the switch would control nothing. The module answers seams of `ui/`, `schedule/` and `general/`, which do not know it exists; removing a setting from `settings.yml` turns it back on for everyone.
- **Tips** (module `schedule/`): messages at an interval, in order or at random — **never the same one twice running** at random, otherwise three tips repeat one time in three. **Off by default**; three examples ship, which only mention the plugin's own commands.
- **Kit layout editor**: `/kit layout <kit>` opens the kit as the player's inventory (storage rows, hotbar, off-hand); they place each item where they want it, and closing the window saves. The kit is then handed out in that order, by `/kit` and by refill signs alike; armour stays worn; `/kit layout <kit> reset` returns to the kit's own order. One layout per player per kit (table `hcf_kit_layouts`), **dropped when staff re-create the kit with other items** — its slots would no longer hold what was arranged. **No item is lost** (`KitLayout`, pure and tested: every item gets a distinct slot whatever the layout says) **and none is duplicated**: the window shows marked copies, only whole-stack moves are allowed in it, and a copy found anywhere else (cursor on close, inventory, login, a dropped item) is destroyed. `layout-editor` in `kits.yml`.
- **Chat format with LuckPerms prefix and suffix and a kill counter** (module `chat/`). The format is **fully configurable** (a template with placeholders): the player's **prefix and suffix** read from LuckPerms when the message is sent (ARCHITECTURE.md section 11), and the **player's kill count next to their name** — the historic HCF format, `[50] PlayerName: message` — from the plugin's own statistics. LuckPerms is a real optional dependency (only `LuckPermsChatMeta` names a LuckPerms type, loaded once its presence is confirmed — the same setup as the Vault bridge). The kill counter is omitted at zero rather than shown as `[0]`; `%message%` is substituted last; a format without `%message%` is refused at load (it would silence the server without an error); local chat by radius, off by default. **What a player types is shown as typed**, `&` codes included, unless they hold `hcfcore.chat.color` (operators by default).

## 10. General utilities

- **Utility commands** (module `general/`, **switchable as a whole**: most servers already run an essentials plugin, and two plugins fighting over `/spawn` help nobody). `/spawn` and `/logout` are **countdowns that any damage or movement cancels** (leaving the block, not turning the head) — `/logout` exists precisely because closing the client in combat *is* an escape, which the combat tag punishes: there has to be a visible, interruptible way to leave. A second countdown is refused while one runs. `/spawn`, `/world` and `/top` ask the `TeleportGuard` seam of `claim/`: a combat tag refuses them. `/kill` goes through `setHealth(0)` so the death follows the normal path (deathban, DTR, stats); `/heal` reads the max-health attribute rather than assuming 20. `/msg` (aliases `tell`, `w`, `whisper`, `m`), `/reply`, `/togglepm`, `/ignore`: someone who turned their messages off cannot send any either (a one-way conversation is worse than none), ignore lists last only for the session, and **vanished staff cannot be found** by `/msg` nor by completion. `/rename`, `/more`, `/ping`, `/heal`, `/kill`, `/gamemode` (alias `/gm`).
- **Everyday commands** (`general/command/BasicsCommand`), what an essentials plugin gives: `/clearinventory` (`/ci`), `/feed`, `/fly`, `/god` (no damage, no hunger, refused at `LOWEST` so a blow on a god tags nobody; for the session), `/flyspeed` and `/walkspeed` (0-10, 1 the game's own - `MoveSpeeds`, pure and tested), `/hat`, `/suicide` (a normal death), `/extinguish`, `/workbench` and `/anvil` (through `MenuType`, the openWorkbench family being deprecated since 1.21.4), `/enderchest`, `/i` (not `/item`: a plugin command takes over a vanilla one of the same name, and `/item replace` is the game's), `/tphere` (`/s`), `/tppos`, `/gmc` `/gms` `/gma` `/gmsp`, `/day` `/night` `/sun` `/rain`. One permission each, `hcfcore.general.<command>`, `.others` to act on somebody else; operators by default. **Left out on purpose**: `/tpa`, `/home`, `/back` (a way out of a fight or a raid) and `/near` (where the enemy is); `/speed` stays the effect command. `ShippedFilesTest` reads every shipped YAML file with the parser Paper ships (SnakeYAML 2.6), and checks each of these commands is declared in plugin.yml.
- **Schedules and custom timers** (module `schedule/`). **Schedules**: announcements and console commands at fixed times, every day, in a configurable time zone — read by `DailySchedule`, already used by the events, so the midnight traps are not rewritten; a time passed while the server was off is not replayed. **Custom timers**: `/timer start <name> <duration> [label]` (alias `/customtimer`), `stop`, `list`; shown on the scoreboard (`%timer_1%` to `%timer_3%`, the soonest first); a name described in `schedule.yml` gets its label and can announce and run commands at the end; a running timer is not restarted by mistake. In memory only, like a running KOTH. Lists ship empty.
- **`/keyall`** runs the `key-all` commands (in `schedule.yml`) once per online player, at once or after a countdown everybody sees (`/keyall 5m`, through the custom timers). **The plugin has no crates**: the commands point at the server's crate plugin, economy or kits. With no command configured, `/keyall` refuses rather than announcing a key-all that gives nothing.
- **The events agenda** (`/events`) lists what runs and what is coming, with the time: KOTH, Citadel, Conquest, Kill the King, DTC, Last Break, Slide and Totem on the capture side, Mountain refills on the resource side, SOTW, EOTW and the Purge. The two families share no engine — family B's lines are *contributed* through the `AgendaContributor` seam rather than owned by `events/` — which gives players one agenda without mixing the engines.

## 11. Anti-exploit and limiters

- **Enchantment, potion and effect limits** (module `limiter/`, **caps ship empty**: Protection I or II, Sharpness I or II, no Strength II… that is the choice that defines an HCF map, not a default to invent). **Enchantments**: a maximum level per enchantment, `0` = forbidden, applied at the table (the offers shown are corrected, and an enchantment with nothing left is refused rather than charged), at the anvil (a result that brings nothing is removed, so nobody pays for a forbidden book), and — `fix-existing-items`, on by default — on what arrives another way (loot, villagers, fishing, other plugins) at login, when an inventory closes and in combat; this net only ever lowers, and the very first hit of such an item can still land at the old level (a hit's damage is computed before its event). **Potions**: a maximum level per effect when it comes from a potion (drunk, thrown, lingering, arrow), brought down to the cap rather than refused; golden apples, beacons and other plugins untouched - the classes' effects included, so `strength: 0` leaves Strength to the Bard. **A forbidden potion (cap 0) is not brewed** (its slot keeps what it held; the ingredient is used up, since cancelling `BrewEvent` restarts the brew and burns fuel each time), and **drinking, throwing or shooting one is refused and the player told** (`PlayerLaunchProjectileEvent#setShouldConsume(false)` keeps a thrown one; `EntityShootBowEvent#setConsumeItem` is deprecated as not functional, so a tipped arrow's shot is only refused). **Effects**: a maximum level per effect **whatever gives it** (`effects.caps`: potion, class, custom enchant, the King, golden apple, beacon, command, another plugin) - `resistance: 3` and nobody has more than Resistance III. The modules that give effects themselves ask first through the `EffectCaps` seam (`util/`, filled by `limiter/`), because the classes and custom enchants recognise their own effects by their level: capping theirs after the fact would make them re-give it forever. Everything else is refused and given again, capped, a tick later. `/hcf reload` and joining bring down what players already have. The rule, `LimiterSettings#allowedLevel`, is pure and tested: for a potion, the lower of both caps. The whole rule is in `LevelCaps`, pure and tested.
- **Blocks per claim**: the maximum count of a block in a team's whole territory — hoppers, spawners, redstone… against lag machines and abuse; placing one more there is refused. `claim-blocks` in `limiters.yml` (**ships empty**, `0` forbids the block; `/team limits` shows the usage; bypass `hcfcore.limiter.bypass` together with `/staffbuild`). **Counting a territory by looking at its blocks would mean loading all its chunks on the main thread on every placement**: each team's part of each claimed chunk (`ClaimCell` - claims being block-precise, two teams can share a chunk, and neither is charged the other's blocks; a recount attributes every block to the team owning its column) therefore keeps its own count (`ClaimBlockCounts`, pure and tested), stored, kept up by placements, breaks, fire and explosions, and **recounted exactly off the main thread** (`ChunkSnapshot`, thread-safe per its javadoc) the first time each claimed chunk loads after a start — which fixes what the events do not see (redstone washed away by water, blocks changed by another plugin). A recount never undoes a placement it did not see: what changes while it runs is added to its result. Pistons are not tracked block by block (where a retraction puts blocks is not to be guessed): their chunks are recounted a few ticks later. Only player teams' land counts.
- **Explosion protection in protected zones**, in `claim/` **and** `resourcenode/` together. Without it, every other protection is optional: a player refused a block break throws TNT instead.
  - **The block list is filtered, the explosion is never cancelled.** A charge placed on a border must take out the unprotected half and leave the other standing. Cancelling the event would do the opposite.
  - **An explosion has no team.** The question is asked as a stranger would ask it — the strict and right reading here: a **raidable** team's territory still blows up (that is exactly what the pillage window is for), a protected team's does not. Attributing the explosion to whoever placed the TNT would be a guess.
  - Both rules live in the managers (`ClaimManager#isExplosionProtected`, `ResourceNodeManager#isExplosionProtected`), so "does TNT break this?" is tested without a server. Set by `claims.yml` (`protection.block-explosions`) and per node (`protection.prevent-explosions`), both `true` by default.
- **Anticlean — loot protection**: for a few seconds after a kill, what the victim drops can be picked up **only by the killer and their team** — no other player, no mob, no hopper — then it is ordinary loot. In `pvp/` (`LootClaim`, pure and tested; `loot-protection:` in `pvp.yml`: 10 s, shared with the team, can be switched off). Drops fall in the same place; the claim is put on the entity before it spawns; player pickup, entity pickup, hoppers and stack merging are all covered. A death with no killer (lava, fall) is not protected; nor are the killer's allies (strictly per team, like captures).

## 12. Configurable vanilla PvP settings

None of these is exposed natively as a global setting by Paper or Spigot (`spigot.yml`, `paper-global.yml`, `paper-world-defaults.yml`): Spigot only exposes a **maximum** for the `movementSpeed` and `attackDamage` attributes, not a global knockback or attack-speed multiplier, and nothing caps enchantments beyond vanilla. They are therefore implemented in the plugin:

- **Knockback**: separate horizontal and vertical multipliers. **Off by default**: it changes how combat feels, so it must be a deliberate choice. Built on Paper's knockback event (`io.papermc.paper.event.entity.EntityKnockbackEvent`), which carries the knockback alone — only the knockback of a player hitting another (weapon, fist, projectile) is scaled, not falling, sprinting or other plugins.
- **Attack speed**: `Attribute.ATTACK_SPEED` (key `attack_speed`). The configured value **replaces the player's base** (the weapon still adds its own modifier), applied as a **transient modifier** — "Transient modifiers are not persisted" — so nothing is written to player files: switching it off or removing the plugin gives vanilla back with no cleanup. The price: it is given again whenever the entity is rebuilt, at login and after respawn (`PlayerPostRespawnEvent`). Off by default, like knockback.
- **Custom enchantment caps**, in `limiter/` (section 11). **"Cap or exceed"**: a cap can be above the vanilla maximum, and the anvil is what reaches it, with the vanilla rule (two equal levels make one more) where the cap replaces the enchantment's maximum — two Sharpness V make Sharpness VI if the cap is 6. Nothing else in the game creates such levels.
- **Classic combat (1.7.10)**, module `pvp/legacy/`: `combat: modern|classic` in `config.yml`, every part switched and tuned in `pvp.yml`'s `legacy-combat`, every value shipped at 1.7.10's. **No attack cooldown** (the attack-speed attribute set high), **no sweep** (its damage and its push refused), **1.7 weapon damage** (a table item → damage, 1.7.10 values read from its item classes - sword 4 + material, axe 3 +, pickaxe 2 +, shovel 1 +, plus the player's 1 - set as the held weapon's `attribute_modifiers`, the attack speed kept, marked by its key so a kit's custom weapon is never touched, reset when switched off), **criticals while sprinting** (the modern game refuses them), **1.7 Sharpness** (1.25 per level against the modern 1 + 0.5 per level above; the blow on a player is rebuilt in one place, `LegacyMath.rebuildHit`, from the modern one taken apart - 26.2 `Player#attack`: the critical multiplies the attack alone, the enchantment bonus is added after - then put back as 1.7 did: weapon, Strength, critical, Sharpness; done in `CombatListener` at `HIGH`, where Strength always was, so the archer tag at `HIGHEST` adds to the 1.7 hit; Power, Punch, Fire Aspect and Flame checked identical in the 26.2 data), **sword blocking** (the `blocks_attacks` item component Paper exposes, set on swords while held and taken back when switched off: `(damage + 1) / 2`, every direction, no delay, no sound, no wear), **1.7 knockback** (velocity halved, pushed and lifted in the air as on the ground; the modern game lifts only a grounded victim - read in the 26.2 sources, where both the hit's push and a sprint's push are named `ENTITY_ATTACK`, told apart by their order), **no off-hand, no shields**, **thrown potions and pearls without the thrower's movement** (the modern game adds it), **no pearl cooldown**, **1.7 natural regeneration** (half a heart every 4 s at 18 food, instead of saturation healing; the game's own is held off through the food bar's regeneration rates - cancelling its heal is not enough, since 26.2 `FoodData#tick` charges the hunger whether the heal happened or not - and given back in modern combat, on logout and when the plugin stops), **1.7 golden apples** (Notch apple Regeneration V), **1.7 Strength** (a percentage of the weapon's damage, +130% per level, where the modern game adds 3 points; with its own HCF nerf, a percentage too - shipped at half, +65%, as `strength-nerf` halves the modern points - since a flat nerf means nothing to a multiplier) and **rodding** (a hook's hit knocks back and tags, and the bobber comes back at once - `remove-hook` - instead of staying on the player, pulling them; reeling a player in is refused through `PlayerFishEvent` `CAUGHT_ENTITY`, which 26.2 fires before `pullEntity`, so a right-click in the tick before the bobber goes pulls nobody). The formulas are pure and tested (`LegacyMath`). The armour formula and Protection stay modern: only through an API Paper deprecates (full Protection IV: 64% today, a random 40-80% in 1.7, 60% on average). Players stay on the current client; old clients need ViaVersion/ViaBackwards/ViaRewind.
- Any other vanilla behaviour a server wants configurable goes through the same check first — official documentation only (CONTRIBUTING.md section 6) — before it is considered as needing custom code.

## 13. Lunar Client integration (Apollo)

Checked against Apollo's official documentation (github.com/LunarClient/Apollo, lunarclient.dev/apollo, MIT, with Bukkit/Paper support).

**A soft dependency.** The plugin works normally without Apollo installed — Apollo features only **improve the experience of players on Lunar Client**, and are never required. A server with no Lunar Client players suffers no degradation.

**Modules used: Waypoint, Team, Cooldown and Nametag** (`integration/lunar/`, `apollo.yml`).

- **Waypoints**: the team's HQ, base and rally; a focused player (where they stand) and a focused team's HQ; KOTH, Citadel and Conquest zones; a running DTC/Last Break's core; a running Slide's zone, a running Totem's column; the King during Kill the King.
- **Team**: teammates marked above their heads and on the minimap, position and name sent beyond tracking distance.
- **Cooldowns**: every cooldown the plugin keeps - the combat tag, the ender pearl, the item cooldowns (Gapple, Crapple…), a running countdown (`/spawn`, `/logout`, `/team hq`, `/team stuck`…), the partner items (each, the shared one, the Pocket Bard's sets), a class's click items and backstab, the crowbar - each with its own item, and each kind switchable in `apollo.yml`.
- **Nametags**: a team line (name, DTR) and a name line, coloured by relation (self, ally, enemy, focus, neutral) — focus wins except for one's own team.

Everything is computed on the main thread every `update-ticks` and **only what changed is sent** (`SentState`, pure and tested, with the nametag rule `NametagStyle`); what was sent is taken back **by name**, never through the modules' `reset*` calls, which would also erase what Apollo or another plugin put there. **It needs Lunar's Apollo plugin (`Apollo-Bukkit`) on the server**: the API is `provided` ("Apollo doesn't require shading") and its accessors throw until the plugin has started; `Apollo-Bukkit` and `Apollo-Folia` are declared as `softdepend`. Without Apollo, nothing changes.

Not used: Border, Combat, Marker/Notification/Title. **Apollo's Hologram module is deliberately not used for the plugin's holograms**: it is visible only to Lunar Client players, which would leave every other player out (section 14).

## 14. Holograms

**Built on Paper's native `TextDisplay` API** (display entities, available since Minecraft 1.19.4 and exposed officially by Paper). **Every player sees them, whatever their client** (unlike Apollo's Hologram module, reserved to Lunar players — section 13), and **nothing needs installing** — no third-party plugin or library.

- **Module `hologram/`**: `/hologram create|addline|setline|removeline|movehere|tp|list|delete` (alias `/holo`), one `TextDisplay` per hologram, facing the player, **stored in the database**. **Displays are never saved into the world** (`setPersistent(false)`) and are recreated when their chunk's entities load: the database is the only copy, so neither a crash, a removed plugin nor a delete made while the chunk was unloaded leaves text floating in a base. Only holograms containing a leaderboard are redrawn (`refresh-seconds`), and each leaderboard is sorted once per drawing.
- **Uses**: leaderboards (`%top_<board>_<rank>_name%` / `_value%`: kills, deaths, kdr, killstreak, playtime; ranks 1 to 100) and static text for spawn boards; and **a hologram above every capture zone, always present** (KOTH, Citadel, every Conquest zone, a DTC/Last Break's core, a Slide's zone): during the event, its status; otherwise, when it runs next (or "not scheduled"). Through a seam, `HologramSource`: `hologram/` draws holograms supplied by code, redrawn every second and never stored or saved into the world; `events/` answers with its zones. `zone-holograms` in `events.yml` (on, height above the zone), texts under `events.hologram.*`. Kill the King has no zone of its own, so no hologram.

## 15. Redeem codes

**Module `redeem/`.** `/redeem <code>` (no completion, which would list the codes); codes are made in game and stored: `/redeemadmin create <code> <max|unlimited> <command>`, `addcommand`, `removecommand`, `setuses`, `info`, `list`, `delete`, and `/resetredeem <code> [player]` to make a code usable again by one player or by all. **The reward is a list of console commands** (`%player%`): an item, money, a rank or a kit, from any plugin — independent of any webstore. **Single-use or multi-player**: every player uses a given code **once**, and a cap bounds the total number of players (1 = single-use code, N, or unlimited) — one setting covers both readings. A code with no reward answers "not ready" instead of being consumed for nothing; **a failed attempt imposes a short wait** (3 s, configurable), otherwise codes could be found by trying names as fast as chat allows. Redemptions are written one by one (a giveaway code can have thousands), and the write is idempotent so a retry after a failure does not block the queue.

## 16. Lives

**Module `lives/`**, **not started at all in kitmap mode** (there is no deathban to lift). A life brings a deathbanned player back, in two ways: a friend spends one of theirs (`/lives revive <player>`, alias `/revive`), or — the only way to use one's own on a single server, where a banned player cannot log in to type a command — it is **spent when they try to connect** (`use-on-login`, on by default). The second path goes through a `pvp/` seam, `DeathbanWaiver`. **A ban "until the end of the map" (EOTW) can never be bought back.** `/lives`, `/lives check <player>`, `/lives send <player> <n>` (can be switched off), and for staff `/lives give|take|set`. Every spend goes through `ConcurrentHashMap#compute`, the rule set by `economy/`: two simultaneous revives cannot spend the same life (a 16-thread load test). Starting lives: 0 — they come from a store, a redeem code or staff.

## 17. Crowbar (End portal frames)

**Module `crowbar/`**: right-click a frame with the crowbar (configurable item, name and lore), `/crowbar give <player> [amount]` to hand it out (staff, a kit, a redeem code, a crate plugin). End portal frames cannot be broken in survival, and are a common part of base traps.

- **The zone rule is in `CrowbarRules`, pure and tested**: the wilderness or the player's own team's claim, **never** an enemy claim — raidable or not — nor server land, nor the warzone. The crowbar lets a team adjust its own traps, never defuse an enemy's during a raid.
- After that, the removal is presented to every other protection as an ordinary `BlockBreakEvent`, which they can cancel (a resource node, another plugin's region): the crowbar gets past a block's unbreakability, not somebody's protection.
- Optional cost (economy) and cooldown, and nothing is paid for a refusal; uses per crowbar (0 = unlimited). The frame drops as an item, and its eye too if it had one. **Ships neutral**: unlimited uses, no cost, no cooldown — those are balance choices.
- **Frames only**: no spawners. **Not handled**: taking a frame out of an already active portal leaves the portal blocks in place, as in vanilla.


## 17b. Elevator signs

**Module `elevator/`, `elevators.yml`**: a sign written `[Elevator]` over `Up` or `Down` takes whoever right-clicks it up or down the sign's column - the classic way between a base's floors. **Elevators in one column are linked** (the owner's rule, 19/09/2026): the next elevator sign that way is where the player goes, whatever lies between - no pillar of blocks - arriving in front of it with the sign at eye level, or at their feet when that is where the room is - two free blocks, or the ride is refused. **Everything is measured from the sign, never from the rider**: counted from the rider's feet, a player a block below an `Up` sign had the floor under the sign - the sign itself - found for them. A sign alone in its column finds the next floor: a block that cannot be walked through (not `Material#isSolid`, which counts a sign as solid and once put riders on top of the sign they clicked) with two free blocks above it, no liquid, no fire; the player's own floor never counts; `max-distance` bounds both searches (`ElevatorRules`, pure and tested). **The sign is marked when it is written** (its block entity's data holds the mark and the way), so text alone never makes one and a sign keeps working if the words change. Using one is a block interaction, read after territory protection: refused on an enemy's land unless it is raidable. A 1-second cooldown; `own-territory-only` and `blocked-in-combat`, both off as shipped. The finished sign's look is `lang/en.yml`'s (`elevator.sign`), themed.
## 18. Classes

**Module `pvpclass/`**, configured in `classes.yml`. A class is chosen by what a player **wears**: the whole armour set of a class — helmet, chestplate, leggings and boots — turns it on after a **warmup** (10 s by default), shown on the scoreboard; taking one piece off turns it off at once, and another set means another warmup. Nothing is stored: a logout or a death drops the class.

- **Shipped**: the five classic HCF classes, with the usual values — **Diamond** (diamond armour, no effects: the ordinary set, named so the scoreboard and `/class` show it; no warmup - a class may have its own `warmup-seconds`), **Bard** (gold: Speed II, Regeneration I, Resistance II; items held in hand buff the teammates within 20 blocks — sugar, blaze powder, iron, ghast tear, feather, magma cream, and gunpowder for Invisibility I (no burst); a right-click spends energy for a stronger burst - no spider eye Wither, removed from the shipped Bard (a click effect with `targets: enemies` still does it); energy +1/s up to 100), **Archer** (leather: Speed III, Resistance II; an arrow hit archer-tags the target, who takes 15% more damage from everybody for 10 s), **Rogue** (chainmail: Speed III, Jump Boost II, Resistance I; a hit from behind with a golden sword is a backstab — three hearts through armour, the sword breaks, 15 s cooldown), **Miner** (iron: Haste II, Night Vision, Fire Resistance, invisible below Y 20).
- **`include-self`** on a held or click effect: whether a team effect reaches its user too (`true` by default). The shipped Bard gives Strength I to the team without taking it (a held blaze powder is `include-self: false`), while its Strength II burst reaches it as well. Refused, with a warning, on a `self` or `enemies` effect, which has no choice.
- **Held effects follow the scroll wheel**: an item's effect comes the moment it is in hand (`PlayerItemHeldEvent`), and is renewed every `held-effect-interval-ticks` (5 ticks, a quarter of a second, as shipped; 1 to 20) by a task of its own, restarted when `/hcf reload` changes the interval - a Bard passes over three items or more in a second. **The warmup and the energy show on the scoreboard** (`%class_line%`, `%class_energy_line%`), not in chat: the warmup message ships empty, and a burst says the energy it cost and what is left.
- **Classes are data, not code.** Every part of a class is optional except its armour, and **any class may use any part** — passive effects, energy, held effects, click effects (with an energy cost, a cooldown, and whether the item is used up), archer tag, backstab, invisibility below a height. A new class is a block of YAML; an unknown item or effect is reported and left out, a class whose set another already uses is skipped. The parsing is pure and tested (`ClassConfig`), as are the warmup, the energy and the team limit (`ClassManager`), the archer tag (`ArcherTags`) and the "from behind" geometry (`Backstab#isBehind`).
- **Targets**: `self`, `team` (the user and teammates in range), `team-and-allies`, `enemies`. The Bard reaches its **team only** by default. **A debuff reaches only a player the user could hit**: it goes through `PvpModule#judgeHarm`, the same judge as a blow (SOTW, safe zones, friendly fire), and skips players the user cannot see (vanished staff) or who are not in survival or adventure.
- **Dyed sets** (`dye-effects`, leather only): per dye colour, a chance for the class's arrows to give the target an effect. Shipped on the Archer, each a 20% chance per hit for 10 s: green (the cactus dye) poisons, black (the ink sac) withers, blue (lapis lazuli) slows. A set has a colour when all four pieces are dyed and each reads as the same dye — the dye its colour is closest to, so a piece dyed with several dyes still counts (`DyeColours`, pure and tested). One dye gives leather exactly the dye's colour, which is what Paper's `DyeColor#getColor` returns (the game's `DyedItemColor#applyDyes` averages the dyes' texture colours; read in the 26.2 sources). **`/dyes`** opens a read-only menu of every colour with an effect — the dye, the class, the effect, its duration and chance, and whether the viewer's set is that colour — and prints the same list to the console. **Dyeing keeps enchantments**: the game's dye recipe rebuilds the piece from the original (`DyeRecipe#assemble`, `createWithOriginalComponents`), so nothing is needed from the plugin; the inventory's crafting grid and the crafting table both work.
- **Held and click effects do not work from a safe zone** (`abilities-in-safe-zones`); passive effects do.
- **Effects never cut a stronger one short.** A passive effect is not put over a stronger one from elsewhere, and only what the class gave is taken back — recognised by its signature (not ambient, no particles), distinct from the custom enchants' (ambient). Pulses and bursts leave the server to keep the stronger effect and restore a weaker, longer one after it.
- **The backstab** is true damage: the hit itself deals nothing and the damage is taken off absorption, then health, once the hit has landed — so armour, enchantments and Resistance do not reduce it. A backstab that would kill is dealt as the hit itself, overwhelming, so the death is the attacker's kill with its deathban, DTR and statistics. *From behind* means on the victim's back side **and** facing the way they face within `max-angle` — walking backwards into somebody's face is not a backstab.
- **Team limits** (`max-per-team`) **ship at 0**, no limit: how many Bards a team may run is a balance decision. A player whose team is full is refused when the warmup ends, and not retried until a piece comes off.
- **`/class`** (your class, energy, warmup), **`/class list`**, **`/class info <class>`**; scoreboard rows `%class_line%`, `%class_energy_line%` and `%archer_tag_line%`, drawn by the module itself through a `ui/` placeholder seam.
- *Not covered*: a class that depends on held weapons rather than armour.

## 19. Effect commands

- **Effect commands** (module `effectcommand/`, `effect-commands.yml`): `/speed`, `/strength`… - an effect **until death**, taken off by typing the command again. Shipped at a Bard's held effects without the burst (Speed II, Strength I, Resistance I, Regeneration I, Jump Boost II, Fire Resistance I, Invisibility I) and the Miner's Haste II and Night Vision, each with aliases (`/sp`, `/str`, `/res`, `/regen`, `/jb`, `/fres`, `/fr`, `/invis`, `/invi`, `/hst`, `/nv`) and a permission, `hcfcore.effect.<command>` by default, operators only. The commands are named by the file, so they are registered through Paper's `LifecycleEvents.COMMANDS` rather than `plugin.yml`: an alias never takes over an existing command (Paper's rule), and the console says which could not be registered; adding or renaming one needs a restart, while `/hcf reload` changes what each gives. The effect is infinite, not ambient, without particles - how a second use recognises it, and one no other module gives (the classes' passive check now refuses an infinite effect: its duration reads -1). Within the effect caps (`EffectCaps`); the potion caps do not apply. Names, aliases, levels and conflicts are checked in `EffectCommandSet`, pure and tested.

## Out of scope

Considered and deliberately left out:

- **Falltraps as a plugin feature.** Players build their traps by hand in survival; there is nothing for the plugin to generate, detect or enforce. The "base token" / "falltrap token" systems that build structures automatically are out as well.
- **Cobblestone generators.** In HCF, TNT cannons are off (building and breaking are refused in a claim until it is raidable), which removes their main historic use as anti-cannon material.
- **Team teleport** (the leader teleporting every member at once).
- **Spawner economy** (removing spawners with the crowbar to sell them).
- **Folia** and **multi-server synchronisation** (ARCHITECTURE.md section 12).
